# Week 18 — Storyboard (Maya catches a spring cold)

**Level:** A2 · **Grammar:** **mir tut … weh**; body parts; **mir geht es gut / nicht gut**; advice with **sollen**; giving a reason with **weil** and its verb-final word order (*Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin*)

| # | stem | scene |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | 01_waking_up_sick | April in Berlin — the spring is here, but the days swing between warm sun and cold grey rain. On Wednesday morning, Maya wakes up and feels terrible (mir geht es nicht gut). She says this sentence here and in one or two later scenes where it does real work — not in every scene, and she does not narrate standing up slowly more than once. She tries to swallow, but her throat hurts, and her head hurts too (mir tut der Kopf weh, mir tut der Hals weh). |
| 2 | 02_calling_in_sick | She takes das Handy and calls Jonas. She tells him that she is staying in die Wohnung today because she is sick (weil ich krank bin). Jonas is sympathetic; he tells her she should stay in bed (du sollst im Bett bleiben) and rest. |
| 3 | 03_booking_appointment | Maya calls the local doctor's practice. The receptionist answers formally (Sie) and asks how she can help. Maya explains that she needs an appointment today because her ears also hurt now (weil mir die Ohren weh tun). The receptionist gives her a slot for half past ten. |
| 4 | 04_reception_desk | Maya dresses, puts on her dark green coat and walks slowly to the practice. At the desk she hands over her Versichertenkarte — that one word for it all week, never *Krankenkarte*. The receptionist processes it without reading the long number aloud, then tells Maya she should take a seat in the waiting room (Sie sollen im Wartezimmer Platz nehmen). |
| 5 | 05_waiting_room | Maya sits in the quiet waiting room with three other sniffling patients. She tries to look at a magazine, but puts it down again because she cannot concentrate (weil ich mich nicht konzentrieren kann). She just leans back and closes her eyes. The scene ends there, waiting — she is not called in until the next scene. |
| 6 | 06_doctor_consultation | The doctor — a man, the same one throughout the week — opens his door and calls her name. He greets her, asks her to sit, and asks how she is doing. Maya replies honestly that she does not feel good at all (mir geht es gar nicht gut). She lists the body parts that hurt, pointing to her head, throat and chest. Then she asks, without much hope, whether she can play badminton tomorrow. |
| 7 | 07_doctor_advice | After a quick examination with a stethoscope and a light, the doctor confirms it is a heavy spring cold. He uses 'sollen' to give strict advice: she should sleep a lot, drink warm tea, and answers that she absolutely should not play tomorrow. He prints a green prescription — the kind for medicine the patient buys herself, which the gloss should make plain. |
| 8 | 08_the_pharmacy | On the way home, Maya stops at the pharmacy. The pharmacist (Sie) reads the prescription, fetches the medicine, and explains how many tablets Maya should take a day. Maya pays a few Euro in cash, takes der Kassenbon, and walks the last few streets home. |
| 9 | 09_sabines_tea | Back in die Wohnung, Maya puts on her pyjamas. Sabine knocks on the door and hands over a thermos of fresh ginger tea; she came over because Maya's cough is audible through the wall (weil du so stark hustest). Sabine tells Maya she should just sleep now, and leaves her to rest. |
