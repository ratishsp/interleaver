Interleaver

Free, open audio language courses that follow one story: Maya, a software engineer from Mexico, moves abroad for a job, and you learn the language by following her year, one week at a time. Each line is spoken in the language you are learning and in English. No app, no screen: press play while you commute or work out.

  • Danish: Maya in Copenhagen (28 weeks, ~4 h)
  • Malayalam: Maya in Kochi (28 weeks, ~4 h)

Both courses currently cover levels A1–A2 (weeks 1–28). 24 more weeks (B1–B2) are planned to complete Maya's year, and more language/city combinations are coming.

Which audio variant should I pick?

First listen: English first, then the target language, so you always know what's being said. Re-listen: target language first, where you try to understand before the English confirms it. Review: target language only: pure immersion, once a week feels familiar.

Flashcards (spaced repetition)

Each course has a free Anki deck with vocabulary, translation, and cloze cards, all voiced with the course audio. Download the .apkg from the releases page and double-click it.

More languages

The same story exists in 21 languages: Danish, English, and 19 more, every line aligned through the English gloss. That allows up to 21 × 20 = 420 ordered pairings of a language you know with a language you want to learn. Pick one:

The 19 tracks are machine-translated. They may sound a bit odd as they are apart from the Copenhagen setting they are translated from. In contrast, the Danish and Malayalam courses are generated source-language-first and thus more realistic sounding.

Build your own language pair

Every synthesized line is published as an individual audio clip (see the clips-cache asset on the releases page). With the repository's scripts and those clips, you can assemble any interleave yourself (either direction, your own pause lengths, any pair of voiced languages) without any text-to-speech account: python combine_all.py --src da --tgt en.

Read along

Every week has a line-aligned transcript: see the course pages.

How it's made

Each week starts from a curriculum row: a theme, a grammar focus, and a story brief. Gemini turns it into a storyboard and then into scenes. Next we have verifiers which are also built on Gemini. Every storyboard passes a four-lens verifier, every scene is verified, every week is reviewed by a three-vote panel, and the whole story is swept for continuity across weeks. A human can intervene at any stage. The full pipeline, prompts, and checks are open in the repo. The audio is synthesized with Google Cloud TTS.
