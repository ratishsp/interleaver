## A1 — Arrival & survival (weeks 1–15): present tense, the immediate world

| Wk | Lvl | Theme | Grammar focus | Brief |
|----|-----|-------|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | A1 | Arrival & introducing yourself | Present of *være/hedde/komme fra*; existential *der er*; subject pronouns; *hvad/hvor* questions | Maya's arrival day in Copenhagen, knowing no one — a grey winter afternoon. Across the day: she lands and meets a stranger, takes a taxi (giving her address), settles into her small flat, takes in the wintry city with a pang for warm Mexico, calls home briefly (a quick audio call — save the family video call, and Lola, for later), and meets her neighbour Nina. The grammar rides on introducing herself and finding her way (*hedder / kommer fra*, *der er*, *hvad/hvor* questions). ~8–10 scenes, varied shapes. |
| 2 | A1 | First day & registration | Numbers 0–100; *har*; *være* + age | Her first days getting official — she starts at the software company (her first day at the office, where she meets her colleague Peter and gets a warm team welcome). Next day, she does the errands that settle her in: getting her Danish phone number, registering for a national ID number at a public office (take a ticket, wait, give her details). The grammar rides on the paperwork: numbers 0–100 (her age *jeg er enogtredive*, a queue-ticket number, the new phone number), *har* (*har du et…? nu har jeg et dansk nummer*), *være* + age (*hvor gammel er du?*). ~8–10 richer scenes. |
| 3 | A1 | Family back home | Possessives *min/din*; indefinite *en/et* | An evening video call home to Mexico — her first proper video call. She greets and talks with each of them in turn: her mother and father, her little sister Sofía, her big brother Diego, and the family dog Lola. The grammar rides on introducing family and home: possessives (*min mor, min lillesøster, min hund*) and indefinite *en/et* (*en hund, et hus*). A little show-and-tell both ways — a glimpse of her new place, a glimpse of home (where *din/dit* comes in: they ask about her flat, she asks about home). A touch homesick. ~8–10 scenes. |
| 4 | A1 | Moving into her own flat | *der ligger/står* (review *der er*); place prepositions *i, på, under, ved* | Moving day — Maya leaves the temporary room for her own permanent flat in the same building; her real home at last. She gets the keys and carries in boxes of her own things, settling into the small furnished flat room by room, deciding where everything goes — with a light touch from Nina. The grammar rides on arranging and describing the flat and place prepositions as she puts things in their spots. |
| 5 | A1 | Food shopping & the kitchen | Definite nouns *-en/-et* — singular and bare (an adjective or a plural takes a different definite form, out of scope); *kan lide* — a general preference takes the indefinite plural; *jeg vil gerne have* | Her cupboards are empty, so Maya stocks her new kitchen. She works the aisles of a self-service supermarket: the produce, where a parsnip she has never seen goes into the basket anyway, out of curiosity; the bread; and the bakery counter, the one place with staff, where she asks for what she wants. She can't find the milk, asks an employee, and is pointed to the dairy aisle. She hunts down one taste of home — a can of black beans — pays at the checkout, carries the bag back, puts it all away in one go, and cooks her first real meal in her own flat. The grammar rides on choosing what she likes and on the putting-away. She goes on her own (shop staff only), and no prices or amounts — money belongs to week 9. |
| 6 | A1 | Settling into a routine | Present-tense regular verbs; reflexive *vasker mig*; V2 review — the verb comes before the subject whenever a time or place phrase opens the sentence | One ordinary weekday for Maya. She wakes up late, has to hurry, washes and dresses, takes the bus to work, and settles at her desk; at midday she eats in the canteen with her colleague Peter; in the evening she cooks, relaxes and goes to bed. No clocks and no frequency words — those belong to week 7. |
| 7 | A1 | Time & appointments | Telling the clock; *klokken*; weekdays/months; frequency adverbs | Planning her week; booking appointments |
| 8 | A1 | Getting to know the city | Imperatives *gå/drej*; direction prepositions | Visiting tourist places |
| 9 | A1 | At the market & prices | Prices; *koster*; *hvor meget* | A morning at a generic outdoor weekend market — Maya goes stall to stall (~8 scenes): coffee, fruit, vegetables, bread, fish, cheese, flowers, candles. Winter-seasonal produce (apples/root veg, not strawberries). Prices — *hvor meget koster det?* / *… kroner*, numbers as words. Vary the shape a little (a taste, a bit thrown in), not eight identical ask-price-pays. No recurring cast — just Maya and the vendors |
| 10 | A1 | Clothes & dressing for winter | Adjective agreement *en/et/*plural; *har på* | A hard cold snap sends Maya shopping for proper winter clothes — her light clothes aren't enough for the deep Danish cold. In one clothing shop she picks warm things — a warm coat, a thick sweater, a soft scarf, a warm hat, gloves, and proper boots — trying them on and choosing what's warm and fits. Grammar rides on the choosing: adjective agreement (*en varm frakke, en tyk trøje, et blødt halstørklæde, en varm hue, varme handsker, varme støvler*), *har på*, sizes/colours; prices as words. Midwinter (cold, no month named). Solo — Maya and the shop assistant. Vary the shapes (trying on, asking a size, a mirror), not item-by-item. |
| 11 | A1 | Danish weather | Impersonal *det er / det bliver*; seasons | Short, dark midwinter days — across one day Maya meets the weather: a dark morning, rain, wind, the early dusk, the cold. She checks the forecast, dresses for it, and remarks on it as Danes do. Grammar: impersonal *det er koldt / det regner / det blæser / det bliver mørkt tidligt*, seasons. Close warm indoors (lamplight and a hot drink — shown, not the word *hygge*). Solo; relative season cues only, no month; vary the moments (window, street, forecast, indoors), not a list of adjectives. |
| 12 | A1 | Free time & meeting people | Verb + infinitive; *godt kan lide at*; *spille/dyrke* | To get active and meet people in the dark winter, Maya joins a badminton club on her own — she finds the club, signs up, sorts out a racket, plays a first game, and arranges to come back, meeting a couple of friendly club members, **Kasper and Sara**. Grammar: verb + infinitive (*jeg kan godt lide at spille, jeg vil gerne prøve at, det er sjovt at*), *spille/dyrke*, free-time verbs. Generic sports hall; no established cast (Nina/Peter) — Kasper and Sara are new faces she meets here. A warm, active close. |
| 13 | A1 | At the café | Modal *vil gerne*; politeness; *må jeg bede om* | A café stop out of the cold, with polite ordering as the focus — Maya greets, orders a drink and a bite, asks for something (a refill, the bill), and thanks the staff. Grammar: *vil gerne*, *må jeg bede om …*, politeness (*tak, værsgo, undskyld*); prices as words. Generic café; solo (Maya and the staff). Vary the exchanges (order, follow-up, pay), not a menu read-out; warm indoor close. |
| 14 | A1 | Public transport | *tage/køre med*; *til/med*; *skal til* | Maya crosses the city by public transport — she buys/tops up a travel card, works out the route, and rides a bus, the metro, and the harbour bus (a water bus), arriving for an errand. Grammar: *tage/køre med* (*tage bussen, køre med metroen / havnebussen*), *til/med*, *skal til*; stops and travel-card amounts as words. Generic transit (a bus, the metro, a harbour water-bus, a card machine — no named lines/stations); solo, plus a ticket machine and a fellow passenger. A warm arrival close on the water. |
| 15 | A1 | **A1 review** — a day in the new city | Mixed present-tense narration; consolidation | A full ordinary weekday, start to finish — an A1 consolidation, no new grammar: morning routine at home, the commute, a stretch at work, an errand or two (shop / café / a bit of transport), and home in the evening for a neighbourly catch-up with **Nina** next door. It recombines the term's grammar naturally (present-tense narration, *klokken*, prices, directions, *har på*, weather, polite ordering, transport). Mostly solo, with Nina's touch to close — her first friend, capping Maya's first months. A warm, settled close — Maya at ease in her new city. ~8–10 scenes. |
