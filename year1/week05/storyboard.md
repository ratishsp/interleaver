# Week 5 — Storyboard (Maya's first big grocery shop)

**Level:** A1 · **Grammar:** definite nouns *-en/-et* (æblet, brødet, osten); *kan (godt) lide*; *jeg vil gerne have*; recurring week 1–4 grammar (present of *være/have/komme fra*,
*der er/står/ligger*, place prepositions *i/på/ved*, possessives, indefinite *en/et*, *hvad/hvor*) ·
**Lines/scene:** ~15 · **Target:** ~36 min.

The curriculum row ("Food shopping & the kitchen — first grocery run, unfamiliar products") is realized
as an **eventful supermarket trip**, in the spirit of Anna's market/supermarket scenes: each scene a
mini-vignette that MOVES — wrestle a cart loose, pick something, ask someone, taste something, the fast
checkout — spanning the aisles, the bakery counter, the till, the walk home, the kitchen. Real
**friction** that resolves: the cart needs a coin, Danish products are unfamiliar, she can't find the
milk, the checkout is fast. Vocabulary is **specific-and-common** (food: æbler, rugbrød, ost, mælk) —
the GOOD kind, not jargon. The grammar lives through the shopping: *definite nouns* as she handles
specific items (æblet, brødet), *kan lide* as she chooses/tastes, *jeg vil gerne have* at the counter.

Realism (Danish supermarket): mostly **self-service** — cheese/milk come from the refrigerated dairy
aisle, not a deli counter; the one manned counter is the **bakery** (so "jeg vil gerne have" happens
there). Carts are chained and need a **coin**. Cultural specifics are kept **translatable** (n-way
parallel data): the sweet she picks is **strawberry jam** (*jordbærmarmelade*, an international cognate —
familiar, not an unfamiliar-product beat), and the one deliberate Danish anchor is **smørrebrød** (kept).
No *leverpostej*/*salt lakrids* (too Nordic to translate cleanly) and no free-sample scene. The odd
vegetable is a simple, widely-known root
(**pastinak**, parsnip) — kept generic so it translates cleanly n-way, not a Nordic-specific one like
celeriac; and not a pepper (Maya is Mexican — peppers are home food, not strange). She names it (reads
the sign) and buys it — a curious, adventurous payoff rather than putting it back.

Continuity (bible): by now Maya is **settled in her own furnished flat** (wk4, in Nina's building), has
a phone + CPR + registration (wk2). This is her **first big weekly shop** at a real supermarket —
distinct from wk4's quick corner-shop top-up. Recurring: family in Mexico (no Nina this week). Season:
still winter. Mood: curious, capable, a little adventurous — warm close.

| # | stem | beat |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | 01_the_list | Morning in Maya's new flat. The fridge and cupboards are still nearly empty — time to stock her own kitchen at last. At her kitchen table she writes a list: milk, bread, cheese, fruit, vegetables. It's her first big grocery run in Denmark. She takes two cloth bags and heads out. (anchors to the new flat from wk4; the list; food nouns *mælk, brød, ost*) |
| 2 | 02_the_cart | Maya walks to the big supermarket — much bigger than the corner shop, bright and full of people. She goes to take a cart, but the carts are chained: she needs a coin. She digs in her pocket, finds a ten-krone coin, and frees a cart. So many shelves, so many things! Where to start? (an authentic friction — the coin-cart; content-neutral overwhelm — the store's abundance, NOT the language; *en indkøbsvogn, en mønt*) |
| 3 | 03_fruit_and_veg | In the fruit and vegetable section Maya picks up an apple. She likes apples and puts the apple in her cart. She takes some bananas, tomatoes, and a cucumber too. Then she sees a vegetable she doesn't recognise; she picks it up, turns it over — "what is that?" — reads the sign (*pastinak*), and decides to try something new: she puts it in her cart. Curious, a little adventurous. (*kan godt lide* as a thought; definite *æblet*/*pastinakken*; *vil gerne* — trying something new; the sign read via *der står*) |
| 4 | 04_so_much_bread | The bread aisle stops her — so many kinds of dark rye bread! In Mexico the bread is soft and white; this Danish *rugbrød* is dark and heavy. She isn't sure which to take, then chooses one. "The Danes love this bread," she thinks, and adds the dark loaf to her cart. (unfamiliar product; *rugbrødet*; a real cultural beat; definite *brødet*) |
| 5 | 05_a_taste_of_home | Maya passes the jam shelves and spots strawberry jam — a familiar treat that reminds her of home. She likes strawberries, takes a jar, and puts it in her cart for her bread. (a warm, familiar comfort — kept translatable, not an unfamiliar-product beat; *der står*, *kan godt lide*, *vil gerne have*, definite *glasset/marmeladen*) |
| 6 | 06_the_bakery | At the in-store bakery counter Maya waits her turn. Fresh rolls and loaves lie behind the glass. "I would like three rolls, please," she says. "Anything else?" asks the baker. "No thank you, that's all." She takes the warm bag of rolls. (*jeg vil gerne have*; the one manned counter; a real exchange; *bollerne*) |
| 7 | 07_where_is_the_milk | Maya can't find the milk. She asks an employee: "Excuse me, where is the milk?" "Over there, by the butter," he says, and points. She walks over to the cold dairy aisle and finds it. She takes a carton of milk, and a block of cheese from the fridge beside it. (friction → help; *hvor er mælken*; definite *mælken, osten*; self-service dairy) |
| 8 | 08_the_checkout | At the checkout Maya puts her things on the belt. The cashier scans them fast — so fast! Maya hurries to pack her bags. "That will be a hundred and fifty kroner," says the cashier. Maya pays with her card and lifts her heavy bags. (the famously fast Danish checkout — friction; numbers; *kassen, betaler, poserne*) |
| 9 | 09_carry_it_home | Outside it is cold and the bags are heavy; Maya stops once to rest her arms. At home she carries everything into the kitchen and puts most of it away — the milk in the fridge — but she leaves the bread and cheese out on the table, because she is hungry. Her kitchen is full now, and she is proud. (brief transition; lunch things stay out; one light definite-noun beat, not an inventory) |
| 10 | 10_first_smorrebrod | Now Maya tries the Danish way. She takes a slice of the dark rugbrød and puts cheese on it — her first *smørrebrød*. Then she spreads the strawberry jam on one of the bakery rolls from sc6 — sweet and good. "Not bad, Denmark," she laughs. (the week's warm close; keeps *smørrebrød* + the cheese; *kan godt lide*; definite *rugbrødet, osten*; jam-on-a-roll pays off both the sc6 rolls and the sc5 jam) |
