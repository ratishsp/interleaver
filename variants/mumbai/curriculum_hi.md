# Hindi · Mumbai — Scope & Sequence (Year 1: A1 → A2, weeks 1–28)

Same story arc as `curriculum_da.md` (Copenhagen) and `variants/kochi/curriculum_ml.md`; the story is
re-instantiated in Mumbai, and the grammar is re-derived for Hindi. Scenes are authored in Hindi.

The **Theme** and **Brief** columns are identical across `curriculum_hi.md`, `curriculum_mr.md` and
`curriculum_sa.md` — one Mumbai story, three language tracks. Edit a brief in one file only via the
sync script, never by hand in a single file. Continuity ground-truth lives in `story_bible.md` (one shared bible for all three tracks).

| Wk | Lvl | Theme | Grammar focus | Brief | Grammar in English |
|----|-----|-------|---------------|----------------|--------------------|
| 1 | A1 | Arrival & introducing yourself | Equational **होना** (है/हूँ) — X is Y and *there is*; subject pronouns **मैं/आप/वह**; **क्या/कहाँ** questions and yes-no questions with fronted **क्या** (क्या आप…?); *मेरा नाम … है* | Maya's arrival day in Mumbai, knowing no one — stepping out of the airport into the mild January night, the sea somewhere in the air. Across the day: she lands and meets a stranger, takes a kaali-peeli taxi (giving her address), settles into her small flat, takes in the enormous unfamiliar city with a pang for Mexico, makes a quick audio call home, and meets her neighbour Asha. | Saying who and what things are — "X is Y" and "there is"; the words for I, you, he and she; asking "what" and "where"; and turning a sentence into a yes-or-no question. |
| 2 | A1 | First day & getting connected | Present habitual **-ता/-ती हूँ** (मैं काम करती हूँ); numbers 0–100 as words; possession **मेरे पास … है** (I have) and its negation **नहीं**; age — **मैं … साल की हूँ** | Her first days getting settled — her first day at the software company, where her colleague Rohan welcomes her and shows her to her desk. She gets an Indian SIM over a lunch break — the form wants her age, and she reads her new number to herself to make sure it is right; the next day she registers at a government office. She gives her new number. The number is spoken only in those two places. A celebratory chai and vada pav when the errands are done. | The present tense; numbers from zero to one hundred; saying "I have" and "I don't have"; and giving your age. |
| 3 | A1 | Family back home | Possessives **मेरा/आपका/उसका** agreeing with the thing possessed (मेरा भाई, मेरी बहन); the indefinite **एक**; **कौन** questions | A slow Sunday shaped by the call home — her first proper video call, morning in Mumbai, evening the day before back in Mexico. Before it, a small errand: she picks up sweets at the corner shop, something to show the family. The call itself: her mother and father, her little sister Sofia, her big brother Diego, and the family dog Lola — a little show-and-tell both ways, a glimpse of her room, a glimpse of home. After the goodbye she walks her street in the Sunday quiet, a touch homesick, the voices still with her. | Possessives — my, your, his and her, agreeing with what is owned; the word for "a"; and asking "who". |
| 4 | A1 | Moving into her own flat | Locatives **में/पर** (कमरे में, मेज़ पर); place words **ऊपर/नीचे/पास**; placement with **रखा है / पड़ा है** (review *there is*) | Moving day — Maya leaves the temporary room for her own permanent flat in the same building; her real home at last. She lets herself in and carries in her bags and suitcases, settling into the small furnished flat room by room, deciding where everything goes — with a light touch from Asha. | Saying where things are — "in", "on", "above", "below" and "near"; and the verbs for things lying and standing in place. |
| 5 | A1 | Food shopping & the kitchen | Wanting and liking with **चाहिए** and **पसंद है** (मुझे … चाहिए / पसंद है); the object marker **को** on people (inanimates stay unmarked) | Her cupboards are empty, so Maya stocks her new kitchen. She works the aisles of a supermarket and the corner kirana shop: rice and spices, vegetables she knows and one she has never seen, what she likes and what she wants for the week — and carries it all home. | Saying "I want" and "I like"; and marking people as the object of a verb. |
| 6 | A1 | Settling into a routine | Present continuous **रहा/रही है** (कर रही हूँ) for actions underway, woven naturally through a habitual **-ती हूँ** routine | One ordinary weekday for Maya. She gets up, washes and dresses, gets her bus, and settles at her desk; at midday she eats with her colleague Rohan; in the evening she cooks, relaxes and goes to bed. | The present continuous — "I am doing" — woven through an ordinary present-tense routine. |
| 7 | A1 | Time & appointments | Clock time **बजे** (कितने बजे?); weekdays & months; frequency adverbs **हमेशा / कभी-कभी / हर दिन** | Maya fills her calendar for the week: the gym on her regular evening, a haircut she books over the phone, a bicycle repair. A coffee with Asha, added midweek, lands at the same hour as another appointment, so she moves one. Not every time is on the hour. No prices — money belongs to week 9. | Clock times; the days of the week and the months; and words like "always", "sometimes" and "every day". |
| 8 | A1 | Getting to know the city | Polite imperatives **जाइए/मुड़िए**; directions **सीधे, बाएँ/दाएँ**; **की ओर** (towards) | A Saturday out in the city. Asha sends her off with a route; from there strangers direct her, and when she gets lost one of them shows her the way back. She reaches the sea face at Marine Drive, watches the water and the long curve of the road a while, and rides the bus home able to picture her part of the city. No prices — money belongs to week 9. | Polite commands like "go" and "turn"; directions — straight, left and right; and saying "towards" a place. |
| 9 | A1 | At the market & prices | Prices — **कितने का है? / कितना हुआ?**; amounts as words; **दीजिए/चाहिए** at the stall | A morning at a busy outdoor market — Maya goes stall to stall for everyday food and a few non-food things. Vary the transactions so no two stalls are the same — a taste offered, an item too dear that she skips, a weighed per-kilo price, change from a note — not the same ask-price-pay each time — and vary how the price arrives too: sometimes she asks, sometimes a board shows it, sometimes the vendor names it unprompted. | Asking prices — "how much is it?"; amounts in words; and "please give me" at the market stall. |
| 10 | A1 | Clothes & dressing for the heat | Adjectives as a system — **अच्छा/बड़ा/छोटा** agreeing in gender and number (and the invariable ones like **लाल**); **पहनना** (wear) | The March heat is arriving, and Maya shops for light cotton clothes. In one clothing shop she tries on and buys a few things — each garment its own complete scene (she picks it up, tries it, decides) — with sizes, colours and prices. One she likes but it is too expensive, so she leaves it. | Adjectives — good, big and small — changing with gender and number, and the ones that never change; and the verb for wearing clothes. |
| 11 | A1 | Mumbai weather | Weather expressions **गर्मी है / धूप है / बारिश हो रही है**; simple future **-एगा/-एगी** for the forecast (बारिश होगी) | Hot season days. One ordinary day told through its weather: Maya wakes to a bright hot morning, checks the forecast, and dresses for the heat. Out in it, the day turns — hard sun on the street, then a sudden evening shower, rare this early in the year, the first rain she has seen here. She closes the day cool at the window. | Talking about the weather — "it is hot", "it is sunny", "it is raining"; and the simple future for the forecast — "it will rain". |
| 12 | A1 | Free time & meeting people | Infinitive **-ना** (खेलना, सीखना) with **पसंद है / जाती हूँ**; ability **सकना** (खेल सकती हूँ) — landing inside varied actions and exchanges, not as declarations | To get active and meet people, Maya joins a local badminton club on her own — she has played for years back home. Club evenings are **Thursdays**; from this week her Thursday gym slot becomes badminton (gym continues Tuesdays). She finds the club, signs up and rents a racket, then joins a doubles game with three friendly players, **Amit, Neha and Sanjay** (new faces), and arranges to come back. | The infinitive — "to play", "to learn" — with "I like to" and "I go to"; and saying "I can". |
| 13 | A1 | At the Irani café | Polite requests — **देंगे? / मिलेगा? / चाहिए** ladder; thanking | A stop at an old Irani café, with polite ordering as the focus — Maya finds a seat under the slow fans, orders a chai and a bun maska at the counter, later asks for one more thing and then the bill, and thanks the staff. Each exchange its own complete scene, with small moments between. | Polite requests — "will you give me?", "could I get?", "I need"; and saying thank you. |
| 14 | A1 | Public transport | **चढ़ना/उतरना** (board/alight); **में/से** with vehicles (बस में, ट्रेन से); **जाना है** (need to get to) | Maya crosses the city by public transport — she buys a ticket, and rides a BEST bus and the metro, changing metro lines, stepping off near the sea for an errand. Solo, plus a ticket-counter worker, a fellow passenger and a shop worker at the errand's end. The metro is the last leg. | Getting on and off buses and metro; "in" and "by" with vehicles; and "I need to get to". |
| 15 | A1 | **A1 review** — a day in the new city | Mixed present-tense narration; consolidation. BOUNDARY: no past tense anywhere — the simple past opens week 16 | A full ordinary weekday, start to finish: morning routine at home, the commute, a stretch at work, an errand or two that echo earlier weeks without repeating them (a shop, a bit of transport, a tourist asking the way), with Rohan at work, and home in the evening for a neighbourly catch-up with **Asha** next door. | Review week — everything so far, in flowing present-tense storytelling. |
| 16 | A2 | Talking about the past | Simple past — intransitives **गया/गई, आया/आई** agreeing with the subject; transitives with **ने** (मैंने देखा); time phrases **कल / पिछले हफ़्ते / जनवरी में** (the perfect *-आ है* is out of scope — week 19 owns it) | After badminton, Amit and Neha ask how she ended up in Mumbai, and Maya tells them: the January arrival, the taxi with her new address, meeting Asha the first day, her first day at the office. On a call home later, Maya mentions the storytelling, and her mother — who lived that goodbye herself — asks to hear Maya's telling of it anyway; the story comes out again, warmer, with her mother adding her own side of the airport morning. | The simple past — "came", "went", "saw" — and the little word "ne" that appears with some of them; and time phrases like "yesterday", "last week" and "in January". |
| 17 | A2 | A trip out of the city | Past-tense narration; **अपना** vs **उसका** (one's own vs another's); time expressions | The heat is high, and Maya takes a weekend train trip up into the hills at Lonavala, told in the past as a travel diary: the train climbing the ghats, people-watching on board, a slow walk to a viewpoint over the valleys, and the ride home tired and happy. | Telling stories in the past tense; "one's own" versus "his" or "hers"; and more time expressions. |
| 18 | A2 | Health & the doctor | **दर्द होना** (मेरे सिर में दर्द है); body parts; **ठीक हूँ / तबीयत ठीक नहीं**; advice with **चाहिए** + infinitive (आपको आराम करना चाहिए) | The change of season brings Maya's first ailment here — a heavy cold as the first rains break the heat. She calls the clinic and books a time, tells the doctor where it hurts and how she has been feeling, and picks up something from the pharmacy on the way home. | Saying something hurts; the parts of the body; "I feel fine" and "I don't feel well"; and giving advice with "you should". |
| 19 | A2 | Work & colleagues | Perfect **-आ है** (मैंने किया है — what she has done); professions | A real project lands at the office, and Maya works closely with the whole team for the first time: Rohan, and the colleagues she knew only by face — the designer **Priya**, the tester **Vikram**, the boss **Suresh** — Priya and Vikram explaining what they do and how long they have done it, Suresh welcoming her aboard. She presents her part at the team's short morning meeting. | The perfect — "I have done"; and the names of professions. |
| 20 | A2 | Volunteering at the café | Past + perfect mixed; **सीखना + -ना** (learning to); ordinals **पहला/दूसरा** | Maya starts volunteering Saturday mornings at a neighbourhood café — to meet people, she takes the counter side of that world. Her first shifts, told as they happened: the experienced hand shows her the coffee machine, her first coffee comes out wrong, her second better. | Past and perfect together; "learning to do" something; and ordinal numbers — first, second, third. |
| 21 | A2 | Buying clothes | Comparative with **से** (इससे बड़ा); **बड़ा/छोटा**; trying on and exchanging | A humid Saturday: Maya shops in town and buys a raincoat and a shirt before the monsoon. At home the shirt is too small. She goes back with the receipt for an exchange: asks for a bigger size, tries the bigger one in the mirror, and it fits better. | Comparisons — "bigger than", "smaller than"; and trying on and exchanging things in a shop. |
| 22 | A2 | Cooking & local food | Imperative sequences; quantities (**आधा किलो, दो चम्मच**); **पहले/फिर/उसके बाद** | Maya cooks a simple Maharashtrian meal for **Neha and Amit**. She finds a recipe, shops for the amounts it calls for, and cooks it step by step. Her friends arrive and they eat together at her small table. Solo cooking, then the shared meal. | Step-by-step instructions; quantities like "half a kilo" and "two spoons"; and "first", "then", "after that". |
| 23 | A2 | Making plans & friends | Future **-एगा/-ऊँगी** and "let's" **चलें/करें**; reporting thoughts with **कि** (मुझे लगता है कि…) | **Priya**, from the project in week 19, invites Maya to her home for dinner. Maya plans the visit — what to bring, when to leave, how to get there, talked through with Rohan at work — and brings something along. Over the meal they talk as friends for the first time. | The future tense — "it will" and "let's"; and reporting thoughts — "I think that…". |
| 24 | A2 | Looking after Asha's dog | Imperatives + object pronouns **इसे/उसे**; rules with **-ना है / मना है** (must / must not) | Asha goes away for a weekend and leaves her dog **Moti** with Maya, with a few rules for his care — when he's fed, what he may and may not have, where he must stay on the lead. Maya looks after him across the weekend: she feeds him, walks him, and calls him back when he wanders off. | Commands with "it" and "him"; and rules — "you must" and "you must not". |
| 25 | A2 | A Sunday in the city | Demonstratives **यह/वह + ये/वे** as a system; **पहले** (ago — दो महीने पहले); time adverbs | A slow Sunday with nowhere to be, the June monsoon coming and going in bursts. Maya wanders a Sunday market, stopping over this and that, then spends an hour out of the rain in a museum. Threaded through it is how long ago things now feel — she thinks back over her arrival and her first months here. | This and that — "this shirt", "that one"; saying "ago" — "two months ago"; and time adverbs. |
| 26 | A2 | The monsoon & her birthday | Perfect **-आ है** vs past **-आ** contrast | Peak monsoon — rain hammering the city for days, and Maya loves it. Two things fall in the same week: the fiercest downpour of the season yet, watched from the balcony with **Rohan and Priya**, and her own birthday — her first away from home; she turns thirty-two. Friends mark the day with her, and she looks back over her first months against the day in front of her. | The difference between "I have done" and "I did". |
| 27 | A2 | Phones, internet & admin online | Habitual **अक्सर/आमतौर पर** + present; instructions; the formal passive **किया जाता है** of official language | Maya updates an account detail on a public self-service website — the small admin everyone puts off. She usually does this on her phone in the evening: she logs in, works through the form, and waits for a one-time code. When the first code never arrives, she works around it and finishes. | Saying what you usually do; following instructions; and the formal passive of official notices. |
| 28 | A2 | **A2 review** — a week in the new life | Consolidation across all A2 grammar | A full ordinary week in the life Maya has built, start to finish — a morning at the office with **Priya**, where the big project is now just work, a badminton evening against **Neha**, a quiet errand, a Saturday café shift, a meal cooked, and dinner with **Asha**. Threaded through, the odd backward glance at how far off January feels. | Review week — everything from the whole course, in one ordinary week of Maya's life. |
