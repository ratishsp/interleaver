# Week 23 — Storyboard (Dinner with Nele)

**Level:** A2 · **Grammar:** The future with **werden**; the dative as an object case (**ich gebe ihr …, ich helfe dir**); **dass**-clauses and their verb-final word order (*Ich denke, dass …*)

| # | stem | scene |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | 01_the_invitation | During a coffee break at the software company, Maya and her project colleague Nele chat in the kitchen. They use the informal 'du', as is standard in their Berlin tech office. Nele casually mentions that she is cooking a big dinner on Friday and invites Maya over to her place. Nele says she hopes that Maya has time, using a 'dass'-clause for the invitation. Maya accepts happily, saying she will definitely come, establishing the future tense with 'werden'. |
| 2 | 02_office_advice | Later that afternoon, Maya stops by Jonas's desk. She tells him that she is visiting Nele and asks what she should bring. Jonas advises her, saying that he knows that Nele loves sweet things ('dass'-clause). He suggests that Maya gives her a nice dessert, prompting the dative object case ('ich gebe ihr'). |
| 3 | 03_planning_the_route | Still standing next to Jonas's desk, Maya pulls out her phone to plan her journey for Friday evening. She sees that Nele lives a bit further out. She tells Jonas that she will take the U-Bahn directly from the office ('werden'). Jonas points out a good connection on her screen, and Maya thanks him for his help ('ich danke dir'). |
| 4 | 04_buying_dessert | Friday after work arrives. Before heading to the U-Bahn station, Maya stops at a charming local bakery. She politely addresses the baker with the formal 'Sie' and buys a small fruit tart. She pays, takes the receipt, and the baker packages the tart in a light, folding cardboard box. Knowing it would be ruined if tilted inside her rucksack, Maya decides to carry the delicate box carefully flat in her hands for the commute. |
| 5 | 05_the_journey_there | Maya rides the U-Bahn across the city. While sitting on the train, resting the bakery box safely on her lap, she sends Nele a quick message on her phone, writing that she will arrive in ten minutes ('werden', combined with a 'dass'-clause). Afterwards, she emerges above ground into the bright, mild late-spring evening and walks the last few streets to the new neighborhood, once again carrying the delicate box carefully flat in both hands. |
| 6 | 06_arrival_and_gifts | Maya arrives at the apartment building and rings the bell. Nele welcomes her warmly at the door. Maya smiles and hands over the bakery box, using the dative directly: 'I brought you something' ('ich bringe dir'). Nele is thrilled with the dessert and invites her inside. |
| 7 | 07_helping_in_the_kitchen | They move into Nele's bright kitchen, where something smells delicious. Nele is still finishing the main course. Maya immediately offers her assistance in the present tense, saying 'I help you' ('ich helfe dir' — triggering the dative case naturally without using 'werden'). Nele gratefully hands her a cutting board and some fresh herbs to chop. |
| 8 | 08_dinner_conversation | They sit down at the small dining table to eat. The conversation naturally shifts away from work projects for the first time. Maya explains that she feels that Berlin is really starting to become home ('dass'-clause). They talk about their lives outside the office, and Maya tells Nele about her weekly badminton club. |
| 9 | 09_future_plans | After sharing the delicious fruit tart, the evening winds down. Maya thanks Nele for the wonderful food and company. As Maya puts on her rucksack to leave, Nele smiles and promises that she will visit Maya's flat next time ('werden', 'dass'-clause). They say goodbye, both happy to have formed a genuine friendship. |
