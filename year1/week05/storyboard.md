# Week 5 — Storyboard (Maya's first big grocery shop)

**Level:** A1 · **Grammar:** definite nouns *-en/-et* (æblet, brødet, osten); *kan (godt) lide* /
*kan ikke lide*; *jeg vil gerne have*; recurring week 1–4 grammar (present of *være/have/komme fra*,
*der er/står/ligger*, place prepositions *i/på/ved*, possessives, indefinite *en/et*, *hvad/hvor*) ·
**Lines/scene:** ~15 · **Target:** ~36 min.

The curriculum row ("Food shopping & the kitchen — first grocery run, unfamiliar products") is realized
as an **eventful supermarket trip**, in the spirit of Anna's market/supermarket scenes: each scene a
mini-vignette that MOVES — wrestle a cart loose, pick something, ask someone, taste something, the fast
checkout — spanning the aisles, the bakery counter, the till, the walk home, the kitchen. Real
**friction** that resolves: the cart needs a coin, Danish products are unfamiliar, she can't find the
milk, the checkout is fast. Vocabulary is **specific-and-common** (food: æbler, rugbrød, ost, mælk) —
the GOOD kind, not jargon. The grammar lives through the shopping: *definite nouns* as she handles
specific items (æblet, brødet), *kan lide* as she chooses/tastes, *jeg vil gerne have* at the counter.

Realism (Danish supermarket): mostly **self-service** — cheese/milk come from the refrigerated dairy
aisle, not a deli counter; the one manned counter is the **bakery** (so "jeg vil gerne have" happens
there). Carts are chained and need a **coin**. The unfamiliar product is a very Danish one
(**leverpostej**, liver paté); the free sample is **salt lakrids** (salty licorice) — the classic thing
newcomers dislike, a perfect *kan ikke lide* beat. The odd vegetable is a **Nordic root** (knoldselleri),
not a pepper (Maya is Mexican — peppers are home food, not strange).

Continuity (bible): by now Maya is **settled in her own furnished flat** (wk4, in Nina's building), has
a phone + CPR + registration (wk2). This is her **first big weekly shop** at a real supermarket —
distinct from wk4's quick corner-shop top-up. Recurring: **Nina** (next door); family in Mexico. Season:
still winter. Mood: curious, capable, a little adventurous — warm close.

| # | stem | beat |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | 01_the_list | Morning in Maya's new flat. The fridge and cupboards are still nearly empty — time to stock her own kitchen at last. At her kitchen table she writes a list: milk, bread, cheese, fruit, vegetables. It's her first big grocery run in Denmark. She takes two cloth bags and heads out. (anchors to the new flat from wk4; the list; food nouns *mælk, brød, ost*) |
| 2 | 02_the_cart | Maya walks to the big supermarket — much bigger than the corner shop, bright and full of people. She goes to take a cart, but the carts are chained: she needs a coin. She digs in her pocket, finds a ten-krone coin, and frees a cart. So many shelves, so many things! Where to start? (an authentic friction — the coin-cart; content-neutral overwhelm — the store's abundance, NOT the language; *en indkøbsvogn, en mønt*) |
| 3 | 03_fruit_and_veg | In the fruit and vegetable section Maya picks up an apple. She likes apples — she smells it and puts the apple in her cart. She takes some tomatoes and a cucumber too. Then she sees a vegetable she doesn't recognise; she picks it up, turns it over — "what is that?" she thinks — and puts it back. Another day. (*kan godt lide* as a thought; definite *æblet*, one apple; an unfamiliar vegetable in everyday words) |
| 4 | 04_so_much_bread | The bread aisle stops her — so many kinds of dark rye bread! In Mexico the bread is soft and white; this Danish *rugbrød* is dark and heavy. She isn't sure which to take, then chooses one. "The Danes love this bread," she thinks, and adds the dark loaf to her cart. (unfamiliar product; *rugbrødet*; a real cultural beat; definite *brødet*) |
| 5 | 05_what_is_this | In the cold section Maya finds a small foil tray she doesn't recognise. What is inside it? A man reaches past her for one, so she asks him: "Excuse me — what is this?" "It's *leverpostej* — liver paté. You put it on rye bread," he says. "It's good!" Curious, she decides to try it. (the man NAMES and EXPLAINS it — no circular read-then-ask; *en bakke*, chilled case; sets up the smørrebrød) |
| 6 | 06_the_bakery | At the in-store bakery counter Maya waits her turn. Fresh rolls and loaves lie behind the glass. "I would like three rolls, please," she says. "Anything else?" asks the baker. "No thank you, that's all." She takes the warm bag of rolls. (*jeg vil gerne have*; the one manned counter; a real exchange; *bollerne*) |
| 7 | 07_where_is_the_milk | Maya can't find the milk. She asks an employee: "Excuse me, where is the milk?" "Over there, by the butter," he says, and points. She walks over to the cold dairy aisle and finds it. She takes a carton of milk, and a block of cheese from the fridge beside it. (friction → help; *hvor er mælken*; definite *mælken, osten*; self-service dairy) |
| 8 | 08_a_taste | By a little table a woman offers a free taste. "Try our liquorice?" Maya takes a small piece — and her face changes: it's salty! Danish salt liquorice. "Do you like it?" the woman smiles. "No... I don't like it!" Maya laughs. Not for her. (a warm stranger moment; *kan ikke lide*; the classic newcomer beat) |
| 9 | 09_the_checkout | At the checkout Maya puts her things on the belt. The cashier scans them fast — so fast! Maya hurries to pack her bags. "That will be a hundred and fifty kroner," says the cashier. Maya pays with her card and lifts her heavy bags. (the famously fast Danish checkout — friction; numbers; *kassen, betaler, poserne*) |
| 10 | 10_carry_it_home | Outside it is cold and the bags are heavy; Maya stops once to rest her arms. At home she carries everything into the kitchen and puts most of it away — the milk in the fridge — but she leaves the bread and cheese out on the table, because she is hungry. Her kitchen is full now, and she is proud. (brief transition; lunch things stay out; one light definite-noun beat, not an inventory) |
| 11 | 11_first_smorrebrod | Now Maya tries the Danish way. She takes a slice of the dark rugbrød and puts cheese on it — her first *smørrebrød*. Then, bravely, she spreads the unfamiliar *leverpostej* on a second slice. She takes a bite... and she likes it! "Not bad, Denmark," she laughs. (the tasting payoff; *kan godt lide* on the unfamiliar food; definite *brødet, osten*) |
| 12 | 12_lunch_with_nina | A knock — it's Nina. Maya opens the door, proud: "Look — my first smørrebrød! Come and eat with me!" She makes a fresh slice for Nina. Nina takes a bite and laughs: "You're becoming Danish, Maya!" They sit in the warm kitchen and eat together. When Nina goes home, Maya looks at her full fridge and her bread on the table — her first big shop, done. She is happy. (the merged social + capable close; present tense "Look", not "I made"; active, not a static recap; *mit køkken*) |
