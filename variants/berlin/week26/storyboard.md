# Week 26 — Storyboard (A Summer Barbecue and a First Berlin Birthday)

**Level:** A2 · **Grammar:** The **Präteritum** of **sein** and **haben** (*war, hatte*) set against the Perfekt

| # | stem | scene |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | 01_hot_morning_commute | Maya takes the U-Bahn to the office on a bright, sweltering Wednesday morning. The morning is already bright and close. Instead of dwelling on the distant past, she uses the Präteritum to explain her immediate morning to herself: she 'had' ('hatte') no time for breakfast because her alarm clock 'was' ('war') too quiet, so she plans to buy something on her way in. |
| 2 | 02_office_bbq_planning | At the software company, Maya chats with her colleagues Jonas and Nele ('du' individually, 'ihr' when addressed together). They decide to take advantage of the warmest evening yet by having a barbecue in the park after work. They use the Perfekt to discuss who 'has organized' what. Maya volunteers to do a quick supermarket run on her lunch break, specifically noting she will only buy snacks and drinks to avoid keeping anything perishable in the heat. |
| 3 | 03_supermarket_errand | During her lunch hour, Maya grabs der Rucksack and heads to a local supermarket to buy potato chips and Brötchen — plural, **die Brötchen**, since three people are eating — for the evening grill. At the till, the cashier ('Sie') asks if she needs the receipt before handing over der Kassenbon. Walking back to the office, Maya grumbles mildly in her own head using the Präteritum — not out loud, and not to the cashier: the queue 'was' ('war') incredibly long, and she 'had' ('hatte') no patience left, because the person in front paid entirely in coins. |
| 4 | 04_park_barbecue | Early evening in the park. Maya, Jonas, and Nele enjoy the heat as the sun stays high in the sky. Over the grill, they mix the Perfekt and Präteritum naturally: Maya tells a story about how she 'had' ('hatte') a massive family barbecue back in Mexico last year, but the food 'was' ('war') much spicier. They relax on the grass, amazed at how late the light lingers. |
| 5 | 05_birthday_morning_surprise | A couple of days later, on Friday, Maya wakes up in die Wohnung on her birthday, turning thirty-two. When she arrives at the office, her team lead Katrin and colleague Sven ('du') have set up a small surprise in the break area. They share cake and drink der Kaffee. Maya is genuinely touched and tells them she 'had' ('hatte') no idea they were planning this, using the Perfekt to thank them for the wonderful morning they 'have organized' for her. |
| 6 | 06_evening_with_sabine | After work, Maya's neighbor Sabine knocks on the door with her small dog Bruno. Sabine ('du') brings a small houseplant as a birthday gift. They sit by the open window enjoying the warm evening breeze. Avoiding heavy nostalgia, Sabine simply asks how Maya's day at the office 'was' ('war') and if she 'had' ('hatte') a good time. Maya replies using a mix of Perfekt and Präteritum to recount her morning surprise. |
| 7 | 07_family_video_call | Later that evening, with the time zone now perfectly aligned for midday in Mexico, Maya sets up das Handy for a video call with her parents, Sofia, Diego and Lola the dog (informal, and **ihr** — she is addressing them all together, not one at a time). They sing to her. Scene 6 has already covered the office surprise — here Maya asks after *them* instead: what Sofia is doing, how Diego is, whether Lola is still stealing shoes. The call is about home, not a third telling of her morning. |
| 8 | 08_thirty_two | Late at night, the summer sky is still a deep, glowing blue rather than fully pitch black. Maya gets ready for bed, putting her things away. She looks back — not over the day, but over the whole stretch since January: the cold arrival, the Anmeldung, the first day at the office, the winter coat, the badminton club. Against all of that, an ordinary summer birthday in a city that is now hers. She reflects simply that she 'had' ('hatte') a wonderful birthday and that the evening 'was' ('war') exactly what she wanted, feeling fully present and happy to be thirty-two. |
